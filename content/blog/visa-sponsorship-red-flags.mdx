If you're job hunting on OPT, CPT, or any visa that requires future sponsorship, the cost of applying to the wrong listing isn't just wasted time — it's wasted hope. Here's what to check before you invest in tailoring a resume.

## Explicit disqualifiers

Phrases like "must be a US citizen," "active security clearance required," or "no sponsorship available, now or in the future" are unambiguous. Filter these out first.

## Soft signals worth noticing

- A role funded by a government contract, even without an explicit citizenship line, often can't sponsor.
- Startups under ~50 employees frequently haven't filed an H-1B before — not disqualifying, but worth a direct question before you apply.
- "US work authorization required" is ambiguous — it sometimes means "must already have it," and sometimes just means "must be legally able to work," which OPT satisfies.

## When it's genuinely unclear

Don't auto-exclude ambiguous listings — many companies simply don't mention sponsorship either way in the posting. It's reasonable to apply and ask directly once you're in contact with a recruiter, rather than assuming the worst from silence.
